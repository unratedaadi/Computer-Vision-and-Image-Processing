Experimental method

OpenCV decodes the image, converts it to grayscale and applies a 5 × 5 Gaussian blur. The fixed method uses intensity 200, selected as a baseline for the bright-background scans. Otsu estimates one threshold per image by maximizing the variance between the two intensity classes. Intensities at or below the chosen threshold become foreground.

Both methods apply a 5 × 5 elliptical opening and closing. Opening removes small spots, while closing fills small gaps. Eight-connected components smaller than 100 pixels are discarded. The area of each retained region is the number of pixels assigned to that component. Its perimeter is traced for the final overlay. Every output uses the benchmark image's pixel resolution.

The benchmark has 17 cases. The five source scans are evaluated at 465 × 638. For each scan, uniform dimming applies a gain of 0.62 and subtracts 12 intensity levels. The side shadow applies a horizontal gain ramp from 0.42 to 1.03. Both preserve leaf geometry. Two composites paste three resized leaf cutouts at different offsets on the same plain background. Reference masks use manually traced polygon outlines. Composite masks use the union of the projected silhouettes, while their reference instance count comes from the number of pasted leaves.

IoU is the intersection of predicted and reference foreground divided by their union. Dice is twice the intersection divided by the sum of foreground areas. Mean absolute area error averages the absolute difference between measured and reference areas as a percentage of reference area. Count matches report the number of images where the component count equals the expected instance count.

IoU and Dice are per-image means within each condition. Raw IoU is measured before opening, closing and component filtering.

  • Original scans. Mean IoU was 0.927 for the fixed threshold and 0.937 for Otsu. Both counted each original leaf correctly. Mask cleanup changed mean IoU only slightly on these scans because most errors are along contours and areas of low contrast.
  • Uniform dimming. Otsu retained 0.937 mean IoU, while the fixed method fell to 0.117. The fixed method marked all 296,670 pixels in each dim scan as foreground. Its count of one matched the expected number despite the failed mask.
  • Side shadow. The leaf and dark background occupied overlapping intensity ranges. Otsu's mean IoU fell to 0.207. More even lighting, illumination correction or local thresholding could address the background gradient. Opening and closing cannot remove a connected background band of this size.
  • Overlap. Otsu detected 3 regions in the separated scene and 1 in the touching scene despite a foreground IoU of 0.977 there. The fixed method returned the same counts. Marker-controlled watershed could help with lightly touching leaves. Heavy overlap may require instance segmentation, and the hidden leaf area cannot be recovered from a single view without additional assumptions.

Limitations. Five related flatbed scans provide a small sample, and the derived images share source objects. Manual boundaries approximate fine tips and petioles. The stress tests simulate lighting changes on the scans. Field performance would need independent, annotated camera images. Converting projected area to physical units requires calibration at the object plane.
